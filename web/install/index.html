Install

Put it on your phone

The route planner works in any browser. On a phone it does more: it follows your position as you ride, speaks the turns so nobody has to look down at a screen, and keeps working where there's no signal.

Which route you take below is decided by the phone you own.

Android

A real app, installed from a file.

Download the app

About 90 MB — most of it is the map, which is why it works offline.

  1. Tap Download the app. Your browser will ask whether to keep the file.
  2. Open it. Android will say it doesn't normally allow installs from your browser, and ask you to turn that on for this one download.
  3. Install, then find Bike Router with your other apps.

About that warning. It appears because the app isn't distributed through the Play Store, not because of anything it does. If you'd rather not, the iPhone instructions work on Android too — open this site in Chrome and choose Install app from the menu.

Updates arrive in the app: it tells you when a new version is out and downloads it the same way.

iPhone & iPad

No app store, no download — Safari can do this itself.

  1. Open pelednoam.github.io/safe-bikes-lanes in Safari. It has to be Safari; other browsers on iOS may not offer the step below.
  2. Tap the Share button — the square with an arrow coming out of the top of it, in the toolbar.
  3. Scroll down the list and choose Add to Home Screen.

You get an icon on your home screen that opens full screen with no browser bars around it, and it works offline once you've ridden or pre-loaded an area. It is the same planner, not a cut-down version.

One iOS quirk. Safari won't let a page speak until you've tapped something, so press Test voice in the app's voice settings once. After that, guidance speaks on its own.

Before your first ride

  • Plan the route at home and tap ⬇ Offline map. That stores the map along it, so the phone doesn't need signal on the road.
  • Let it use your location when it asks — that's how it follows you and knows when to call a turn. Your position never leaves the phone.
  • Nothing you save is sent anywhere. Routes, the streets you've marked as sketchy, and hazard photos all stay on the device.

Not sure yet? Try it in this browser first — it's the same planner. Or read what it says about Somerville, Cambridge or Lexington.
